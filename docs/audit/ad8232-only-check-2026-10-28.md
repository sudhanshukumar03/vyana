# AD8232-only electrical bench check 28 October 2026

The user confirmed that only AD8232 is directly connected to the ESP32 and that
electrodes are off the body while computer USB is connected. Port enumeration
identified a CP210x bridge on COM3. An initial 18-second serial read found the
previous MAX30102-only firmware, which was not sampling AD8232.

Added an explicit `ad8232_bench` PlatformIO environment using the existing AD8232
driver. It excludes networking, private configuration and all other sensor
drivers. The normal default and production acquisition driver are unchanged.
The bench reports raw CSV samples and one-second JSON summaries, with counts
covering samples even when lead-off is indicated. It does not classify rhythm.

## Build and physical observations

- The pinned ESP32 build succeeded. Upload identified ESP32-D0WD revision 1.0 on
  COM3; flash hashes verified and reset completed.
- A 25-second baseline captured 6,186 raw samples and 25 summary records, with
  no malformed lines. Raw ADC minimum, maximum and mean were all zero.
- All 6,186 samples met the clipping criterion (raw <=5 or >=4090): 100% clipped.
- Both digital lead pins were zero in all summary records; no sampled lead-off
  indication was observed. The firmware therefore displayed `Unknown`, which
  only means unclassified and cannot prove contact or module presence.
- Observed sample rate was 247.49 Hz; median timestamp gap was 4 ms and maximum
  was 15 ms. These describe this short run, not a guaranteed recorder rate.
- The last separate calibrated ADC conversion was 142 mV. This is not the same
  conversion as the raw sample and does not replace a physical voltage check.

The capture confirms firmware acquisition and serial output, but **does not
confirm a functioning AD8232 signal path or a usable ECG**. An open-input bench
cannot establish physiological performance. Flat input can have several causes;
the present evidence does not isolate power, pin mapping, shutdown, cable or
module failure. No hardware fault was diagnosed from ADC counts alone.

## Wiring confirmation and repeat

With USB disconnected, verify 3.3V→3V3, GND→GND, OUT→GPIO34, LO+→GPIO35,
LO−→GPIO32. If exposed and not already biased by the breakout, SDN must be held
at 3.3 V for normal operation. Keep electrodes off the body when reconnecting
computer USB. The user subsequently confirmed that all connections match.

An 18-second repeat captured 4,453 samples and 18 summaries, with no malformed
lines. ADC minimum/maximum remained 0/0, all samples were clipped, and both
lead pins remained LOW. Observed rate was 247.50 Hz, median gap 4 ms and maximum
gap 15 ms. These results reproduce the flat input after the wiring confirmation;
they do not isolate a defective component. Availability of a multimeter or spare
jumper was requested for the next physical power/ADC check.

## ADC jumper and shutdown follow-up

The user had a spare jumper but no multimeter. Instructions were to unplug USB,
disconnect AD8232 OUT from GPIO34, and connect GPIO34 to the board's 3V3 pin.
After the user reported ready, a ten-second capture recorded 2,474 samples with
range 0–4095 and mean 638.35; 90.54% were clipped. A second ten-second capture
with the connection left untouched recorded 2,473 samples, all zero. Thus the
reference connection was not established as a stable high input. This is not a
passed ADC test and does not identify a defective ADC or AD8232. Files use the
prefixes `adc-3v3-reference-` and `adc-3v3-held-`.

The user then asked whether SDN needs a connection. The datasheet requires SDN
HIGH for normal operation (LOW selects shutdown). Instructions were to unplug
USB, remove the temporary GPIO34-to-3V3 jumper, restore AD8232 OUT→GPIO34 and tie
exposed SDN to 3V3 before reconnecting USB with electrodes off the body.
The earlier general wiring confirmation did not establish that SDN was actually
HIGH. The user then confirmed that the requested connections were completed.

## After SDN connection and restored OUT

A 20-second capture after that confirmation recorded 4,930 samples and 20 JSON
summaries, with no malformed lines. Raw ADC varied from 0 to 4095, with mean
2019.22. A total of 3,991 samples (80.95%) were clipped. LO+ read LOW and LO−
read HIGH in every summary, and all sampled combined lead indications were
`Leads Off`. Observed rate was 246.50 Hz, median timestamp gap 4 ms and maximum
gap 24 ms. Files use the prefix `sdn-high-restored-out-`.

The changed input and asserted lead-off indication are consistent with a
responsive electrical acquisition path after the reported SDN wiring change.
The physical SDN voltage was not measured, and the change does not establish
signal accuracy or a complete module qualification. With electrodes off the
body, this heavily clipped open-input capture is not a usable ECG and must not
be presented as a heartbeat waveform or used to calculate heart rate.

At that checkpoint, the user reported only computer USB power, with no
standalone power bank. No body-connected measurement was collected then.
The board was left running `ad8232_bench` pending an appropriate wireless setup.

## Correction to external SDN wiring advice

The user questioned the instruction to connect SDN externally. Inspection of
the SparkFun v10 schematic confirmed R15 (10 kΩ) pulls SDN to 3.3V; its hookup
guide marks SDN as unused. An external SDN wire is unnecessary for that design.
Earlier wording treating the external wire as mandatory was incorrect. The
chip needs a HIGH logic level, which an onboard pull-up can already provide.
The user's exact breakout revision has not been independently identified.

Guides now distinguish the onboard pull-up from an external enable wire.
The wireless bench does not drive any SDN GPIO. Its source startup message
was corrected; no firmware behavior was changed and no new upload was made
for this wording correction. A correctly tied 3V3 connection also holds SDN
HIGH, so these captures do not establish that the external wire caused clipping
or that removing it would fix the ECG. Any wiring change must be made with
pads removed and battery power off. The earlier observation after the reported
SDN wiring change remains a temporal association, not proof of causation.
